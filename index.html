How the constructed flow blows up

The fluid starts from absolute rest

u(x,0) = 0. Nothing moves. No initial “kick” — the whole event is driven by an external force. (This is why the theorem uses alternatives (C)/(D) of the Clay statement, which permit a smooth force.)

A tame force is switched on

f ∈ C∞c(ℝ³×(0,∞)) — smooth, compactly supported in space, and its support lies strictly in positive time. It is a time-limited “control” that begins and ends cleanly. Toggle it off in the sim: no force, no motion — the fluid simply stays at rest.

Self-similar collapse

With τ = 1−t: core radius ℓ_r ~ τ^½, half-length ℓ_z ~ τ^(½−h) (slendering column), swirl u_θ ~ τ^(−½−h) → ∞. Radial inflow carries angular momentum r·u_θ inward — conservation spins the core up, exactly like ice skaters pulling in their arms.

Incompressibility forces axial outflow

Fluid cannot pile up on the axis. The divergence-free constraint turns inflow into outflow through the top and bottom of the column (with a small asymmetry across the dividing layer at z = 0). The angular Reynolds number Re_θ ~ τ^(−h) → ∞: the fluid makes more and more turns per diffusion time.

Waves cancel the annulus residual

Between the collapsing core and the calm exterior lies an annulus with an unbounded momentum residual. Oscillatory pulses (convex-integration / h-principle, Buckmaster–Vicol style), iterated at every order (the 166-page, 641K-line construction), cancel it — leaving a force that stays smooth through t = 1.

Energy bounded, velocity infinite, no escape

Total energy ∫|u|² dx < ∞ for all t (core energy even ~ τ^(½−3h) → 0), but ‖u‖_L∞ ~ τ^(−½−h) → ∞ at t = 1. Any global smooth finite-energy solution would have to coincide with this flow on [0,1) (comparison, §10) and then fail to extend. No global solution exists. ∎
